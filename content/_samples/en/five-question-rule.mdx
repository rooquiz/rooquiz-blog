Ask a coach how many questions their lead quiz should have and you will hear "as many as it takes." Look at the completion curves and you will see something narrower: **after the fifth question, each additional one costs roughly 3–5% of completions**, and almost none of them change which outcome the person lands in.

## Which five survive

Score every candidate question on two axes:

- **Discrimination** — does the answer actually split people across outcomes?
- **Self-recognition** — does answering it make the person feel seen?

A question that scores low on both is decoration. A question that scores high on discrimination but low on self-recognition belongs in the follow-up email, not the quiz.

## The test

Run your question list through this and cut anything that fails:

- Remove the question. Does anyone's outcome change? If no, cut it.
- Would a stranger's answer be obvious from their answer to another question? If yes, cut it.

Most 12-question quizzes collapse to six this way. Cut the sixth too.
